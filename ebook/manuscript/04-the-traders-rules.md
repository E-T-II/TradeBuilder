# The trader's rules

> **Status:** outline

The discipline rules sit before the method on purpose. They are the ones that
get broken first, and breaking them makes the rest of the book useless.

## The rules, as written

> Deviation is not in the program. Don't do it!

- [ ] Never invest more than **50%** of the account balance in one trade.
- [ ] Never risk more than **6%** of the account balance across multiple open
      trades.
- [ ] **If we did not score the trade, we will not take the trade.**
- [ ] If we are angry, we will not trade.
- [ ] If we are excited and feeling good (cocky), we will not trade.
- [ ] Hope, fear and greed are not a strategy.

## Must cover

- [ ] Each rule restated in the reader's own decision terms: what it forbids, at
      the moment they would break it.
- [ ] Why the emotional rules are listed alongside the arithmetic ones — they
      fail the same way, by substituting a feeling for a number.
- [ ] The self-check: if a decision is being made on hope, fear or greed, stop.
- [ ] What "we did not score the trade" looks like in practice — taking a setup
      because it *looks* like yesterday's winner.
- [ ] The two rules that are enforced mechanically later (50% capital cap, 6%
      total risk) and the ones only the reader can enforce.

## Notes

The 50% and 6% rules reappear as arithmetic in the risk-management chapter. Here
they are stated as commitments; there they are computed. Don't duplicate the
arithmetic in this chapter — cross-reference it.
