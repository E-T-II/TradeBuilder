# Worked examples — when the answer is no trade

> **Status:** outline

A chapter of rejections. Most setups a reader looks at should end here, and the
discipline of the strategy is in accepting that quickly.

## Must cover — one worked example each

- [ ] **Matrix veto.** A demand zone high on the curve in a downtrend (rule d).
      Score it anyway to show a strong score being overruled.
- [ ] **Score below 7.** A setup that passes the matrix but scores 6.5. Show how
      close it can look.
- [ ] **Zones too tight.** The target buffer lands the target on the wrong side
      of the entry.
- [ ] **Reward:risk below 3:1.** Show the ratio it reaches and why widening the
      target is not permitted to fix it.
- [ ] **Risk budget too small.** One share's risk exceeds the 2% allowance, so
      the size rounds to zero.
- [ ] **One share over the capital cap.** A high-priced stock against a small
      balance.
- [ ] **The 6% ceiling.** A good setup rejected because of trades already open.

## For each example

- [ ] The inputs.
- [ ] Where in the process it fails.
- [ ] What the reader should do next — including when the *opposite* direction
      is worth checking.
- [ ] What the reader should **not** do: adjust the zones, inflate a judged
      score, or take it smaller to sneak under a hard rule.

## Notes

These seven map exactly onto the app's seven no-trade reasons. Keeping the book
and the app aligned here is what makes the app's explanations trustworthy.
